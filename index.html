About the data

Where the numbers come from and how to read them.

One example family

All money is shown for the same kind of family in every country: two working parents expecting their first baby. The mother earns two-thirds of the country’s average wage, her partner earns the average. Amounts are in US dollars at the 2025 exchange rate.

How the money is added up

  • State support: birth payment, child allowance and tax break for 3 years, baby bonus, and pay during leave for mother and father.
  • Cost of a baby: giving birth, child health insurance for 3 years and 2 years of daycare.
  • What’s left after costs: support minus costs; shown as a share of the family’s income over 3 years, or per month.
  • Mother’s workdays for daycare: how many of her working days each month pay the daycare bill.
  • Hungary’s baby loan is a loan, so it is not counted. Russia’s maternity capital is counted, but can only be spent on housing, education or pension.

Reading the numbers

  • Every number links to its source.
  • estimate · to verify · * there is a caveat: hover or tap the mark.
  • no data means the value is missing, never zero.
  • Link with births: from −1 (more of this, fewer babies) to +1 (more of this, more babies). Spearman rank correlation with children per woman across the countries that have data; near 0 = no link.

Read with care

  • Linked does not mean caused. Countries often start paying more because births have already fallen.
  • Model calculations. Most money values are estimates from national rules for one example family; real families differ.
  • Surveys are worded differently. EU: “family life suffers when the mother works full-time” (Eurobarometer 2024); elsewhere: “a pre-school child suffers” (national surveys, World Values Survey). No survey for Israel.
  • Some values are measured differently: grandparent care in Japan, Russia and China, housework in Czechia and China, working mothers in Norway, Korea, Israel and Russia (other age groups). The * next to the value explains.
  • State family benefits count cash only (allowances, leave pay, birth payments), not daycare or tax breaks; Russia and China are estimates, no figure for Kazakhstan.
  • 27 countries only. One country can change a ranking.

Sources in short

Children per woman: World Bank, national statistics. Leave, daycare, state spending, working mothers, births outside marriage: OECD Family Database. Housework: national time-use surveys. Attitudes: Eurobarometer 545 (2024), national surveys, World Values Survey. Money rules: government sources for each country. Exchange rates: World Bank, central banks of Israel and Kazakhstan.
